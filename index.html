Informed Consent to play the educational computer game "SCAMS"

The purpose of this educational computer game "SCAMS" is to help students understand fundamental cybersecurity concepts, stimulate their interest in cybersecurity, and motivate them to continue studying cybersecurity.
Participating in this educational computer game is optional. Players can play as guests in anonymous mode or create their account using fake names (pseudonyms or nicknames). Players should NOT enter any Personally Identifiable Information (PII), such as bank account information, Social Security numbers, personal addresses, or student IDs, in the SCAMS game, such that in the event of a data breach, no PII data or FERPA-protected data is leaked. In this computer game, you will be solving cybersecurity challenges and answering questions. Players can cease their participation, decline to answer specific questions, and exit the game at any time they want. It will take about 5-30 minutes to complete each game module, depending on the module.
This game is followed by an evaluation survey that collects data on your student status (freshman, sophomore, junior, senior, or graduate) and major.
There is one anticipated risk with this experiment (computer game). Some of the game challenges may ask about cyber and social engineering attacks. If a player was a victim of a cyber or social engineering attack in the past, the game may cause the player to experience a Post-traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) attack.
Do you agree that you understand this information? Agreement is required to play the game.
